# Basics tab

Everything on this tab is what people **see**. None of it changes how the persona thinks — that's
the [Prompt tab](/admin/handbook/persona-prompt). You can change all of it later without
affecting any conversation in progress.

| Field | Required | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Yes | Shown everywhere. Also what people @mention in a room. |
| **Slug** | No | The web address: `/personas/numera`. Leave blank and it's generated from the name. |
| **Tagline** | No | One line under the name on the catalogue card. |
| **Expertise** | No | Two or three words. Shown on the card and in the chat header. |
| **Description** | No | A paragraph on the persona's own page. |
| **Accent colour** | Yes | Colour of the avatar tile and the header on its page. |

## Choosing a name

Short names work better than descriptive ones. `Numera` beats `Primary Maths Tutor Assistant` for
three practical reasons: it fits on a card, it's quicker to @mention in a room, and it reads like a
colleague rather than a tool.

## A warning about slugs

Once a persona is live and people have bookmarked or linked it, **changing the slug breaks those
links**. The old address stops working. Set it once when you create the persona and leave it alone.

## Tagline vs description

- **Tagline** is a hook — it appears next to a dozen other cards. *"Maths, explained without the panic."*
- **Description** is the detail someone reads once they're interested. Who it's for, what it covers,
  what it won't do.

> **Good pairing.**
> Tagline: *"Contract review, in plain English."*
> Description: *"Reviews commercial contracts and explains what each clause actually means for you.
> Covers UK law. Not a substitute for advice from a solicitor on anything high-value."*

That description sets an expectation and a limit. Both are useful.
