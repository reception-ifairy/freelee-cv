# How a persona looks

Every persona on this site is shown as a **card with a generated mark** — a small geometric pattern,
built from what the persona *is*. Never a photograph, and never an AI-generated face.

That is a deliberate rule, not a gap waiting to be filled. A face makes a promise the product cannot
keep: it invites people to relate to a colleague who does not exist. A mark says *specialist*, which
is what you are actually offering.

## Where the mark comes from

You do not draw it or upload it. It is generated from three things, and it never changes on its own:

| What you set | What it changes |
|---|---|
| **Category** | The colour, and the shape of the cells — squares, circles, cut corners or diamonds |
| **Sector** | How dense the pattern is |
| **The persona itself** | Which cells are filled |

The useful consequence: **two personas in the same sector look like relatives** — same rhythm, same
family — while still being individually recognisable. A visitor scanning the catalogue can see the
shape of your line-up before reading a single word.

Change a persona's category or sector and its mark changes with it. Rename the persona and nothing
changes, because the mark is built from the slug, not the display name.

> **Accent colour still matters** — it colours the persona's own page and its chat header. It just no
> longer stands in for a picture.

## The flip

Every card turns over. Click the arrow in the corner, or focus it and press Enter.

- **Front — the credential.** Category and sector, name, expertise, who it is for, which model does
  the thinking, and the tools it can actually call.
- **Back — access and depth.** What it knows about, what it can do beyond text, how many safeguards
  are active, what a message costs, and the way in.

Nothing is hidden behind the flip that a visitor *needs* — the front is enough to choose. The back is
for the person who wants to know more before they start.

If someone has asked their device to reduce motion, the card swaps sides instantly instead of
turning. Nothing is lost.

## Categories and sectors, in one place

**Admin → AI → Taxonomy.** Categories and sectors used to be two separate screens; they are now one,
because a sector only means anything inside its category.

A category is the field — *Marketing and advertising*, *Legal*, *Health*. A sector is the speciality
inside it — *Search engine optimisation*, *Contract review*. Each sector carries scores for how well
AI suits that kind of work, which is worth reading before you build a persona for it.

Set both on the persona's Basics tab. A persona with no sector still gets a mark; it just has one
fewer thing making it distinctive.

> **One thing not to do.** Renaming a category is safe. Its **address** (`/personas?category=legal`)
> is set once when you create it and deliberately cannot be changed afterwards — several behaviours,
> including which chat layout a persona gets, are keyed to it. If a category's address is genuinely
> wrong, make a new category and move the personas across.

## Assigning personas by dragging

On a bot team's page the members are cards too, in two columns: everyone available on the left, the
team on the right.

**Drag a card across** to add it, drag it back to remove it, and drag within the right-hand column to
change the running order — which in pipeline mode is the order they act in.

Every card also has a plain **+** and **×** button. Dragging is faster; the buttons always work, and
work with a keyboard.
