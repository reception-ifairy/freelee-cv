# Personality tab

Ten sliders, each 0 to 100, all starting at 50. They're written into the prompt as instructions
like *"Patience: very high (95/100)"*.

They **nudge** tone. They do not override your system prompt. If your prompt says "be brief" and
you set Storytelling to 100, the prompt wins — and you've made the instructions contradict each
other, which never improves anything.

| Trait | Low (0–30) | High (70–100) |
|---|---|---|
| **Warmth** | Businesslike, neutral | Kind, personal, checks how you're doing |
| **Humour** | Completely straight | Jokes and light asides |
| **Formality** | "Yeah, that'll work" | "That would be advisable" |
| **Curiosity** | Answers exactly what was asked | Asks about your wider goal |
| **Patience** | Assumes you keep up | Re-explains as often as needed |
| **Directness** | Suggests, hedges, offers options | Gives you the answer, plainly |
| **Creativity** | Conventional, safe | Unusual angles |
| **Rigour** | Broad strokes | Precise, cites, caveats |
| **Encouragement** | Neutral | Actively supportive |
| **Storytelling** | Facts and lists | Examples, analogies, scenarios |

## How to actually use these

**Set three or four. Leave the rest at 50.**

Turning every slider to 100 doesn't produce an ideal persona — it produces a shapeless one. A
character is defined as much by what it *isn't*. Numera the tutor is patient and warm **and
deliberately not direct**, because directness would mean handing over homework answers.

### Worked examples

**Children's tutor** — Patience 95, Warmth 85, Encouragement 90, Formality 15, **Directness 30**

**Contract reviewer** — Rigour 95, Directness 85, Formality 75, Warmth 30, **Humour 0**

**Brainstorming partner** — Creativity 95, Curiosity 90, Storytelling 80, Rigour 25

**Support desk** — Directness 90, Warmth 60, Rigour 70, Storytelling 10

Notice each one has at least one deliberately *low* setting. That's what makes them distinct from
each other.
