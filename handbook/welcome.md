# What this platform does

You run a small agency of AI specialists.

Each specialist is called a **persona**. A persona is not a different AI — underneath, they all
use the same handful of AI models. What makes them different is everything *you* write around
that model: what it knows, how it talks, what it refuses to do, and what it looks like when
someone chats with it.

Think of it like hiring. Two people can have the same degree and be completely different
colleagues. One is blunt and fast, one is patient and explains everything. That difference isn't
their education — it's them. On this platform, you are the one who decides who each persona is.

## The words you will see everywhere

| Word | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | One AI specialist. "Numera the maths tutor." Has its own name, instructions and personality. |
| **Chat** | One person talking to one persona, privately. |
| **Room** | Several people *and* several personas in one conversation together. |
| **Crew** | Several personas working through a task on their own, without a human in the loop. |
| **Credits** | What a message costs. Your customers buy them; every reply spends some. |
| **Category** | A subject area, like "Legal and Compliance". Used to organise your catalogue and to pick a sensible chat layout. |
| **Model** | The actual AI engine doing the thinking — GPT-4o, Claude, and so on. |

## What you control, in order of how much it matters

1. **The system prompt** — the instructions that define a persona. This has more effect than
   everything else on this list combined. A brilliant prompt on a cheap model beats a lazy prompt
   on an expensive one, most days.
2. **The model and its settings** — how capable and how creative the underlying AI is.
3. **Personality and guardrails** — the tone, and the lines it must not cross.
4. **The interface** — what the chat window looks like and which buttons exist.

Most people new to this reach for the model settings first. That's the wrong end. If a persona is
giving bad answers, the prompt is the first thing to look at, nearly every time.
