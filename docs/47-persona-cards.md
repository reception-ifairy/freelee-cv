# Persona cards: a generated identity

A milestone decision about what an AI specialist *looks like* here, and a standing rule:
**a persona is never represented by a human face.** Not a stock photo, not a generated portrait, not
an illustrated mascot. An AI specialist is not a person, and dressing one up as one makes a promise
the product cannot keep.

## The brief was half true

The audit reframed it usefully: **there were no fake AI people to remove.** Every persona visual on
the site was `initialsOf(name)` on `accentColor` — two fields, in eight places. `personas.avatar`
exists in the schema, has **never been written and never rendered on a card**, and there is no
avatar service anywhere (`ui-avatars`, `picsum`, `dicebear` — zero hits).

So this was not a removal job. It was a mandate never to introduce them, and to build something
better than two letters — while everything that could describe a specialist sat unused: ten
personality axes, twelve capability flags, guardrails, knowledge domains, tools, model tier.

## The mark

`src/lib/persona/mark.ts` — pure, deterministic SVG. No dependency, no network, no image files.

| Axis | Drives |
|---|---|
| **Category** | The field colour, and the cell geometry — square, circle, chamfer or diamond |
| **Sector** | Grid density and rhythm, so two specialists in one sector are visibly relatives |
| **The persona** | Which cells fill, so relatives are still individuals |

Symmetric about the vertical axis, which is the whole difference between a mark and a mess: random
cells read as noise, mirrored cells read as something made on purpose.

Inline SVG rather than an `<img>` for three reasons that all matter — no request, it inherits theme
colours, and **there is nowhere to put a photograph.** The rule is easier to keep when the component
is incapable of breaking it.

### Three things the property tests caught

**Shape distribution was broken.** `hash % 4` over the real 20 category slugs gave 9 squares and 1
circle — half the catalogue sharing a silhouette, which defeats having one. Adding a murmur3
avalanche moved it to 10 circles and 1 chamfer: no better, because *a hash cannot guarantee spread
across a set as small as twenty*. Shape now comes from the category's own sequential id, which cycles
the four shapes exactly evenly: **5/5/5/5**.

**The empty-mark fallback could never satisfy its own condition.** It triggered below `grid` filled
cells and lit only the centre column's inner rows — `grid - 2` cells. It is now a cross. By eye it
looked fine; only the property test found it.

**`personas.avatar` stays.** Its one live consumer is the site-assistant bubble, where a brand avatar
is legitimate. Removing the column would break that for no gain — the rule is enforced where it
matters, in the card.

## The card

Front is a **credential**: mark, category · sector, name, expertise, audience, model tier, and the
tools it can actually call. Back is **access and depth**: knowledge domains, capabilities, safeguards,
price, and the way in.

### What it had to get right, and got wrong first

**The stretched link had to go.** The old card made the whole surface one anchor
(`after:absolute after:inset-0`), which would have swallowed both the flip button and the drag
handle.

**Flip on click, not hover** — hover is unusable on touch and fires constantly while scanning a grid.
The hidden face is `inert`, so a grid of cards does not double the number of tab stops with half of
them invisible.

**Three layout bugs, all found by measuring rather than looking:**

- The scene needed `min-w-0`. A grid item defaults to `min-width: auto` and refuses to shrink below
  its content, so one long category name widened its column and pushed the grid past the viewport.
- The faces needed an explicit `grid-cols-1`. An implicit `auto` column grows to the widest item —
  the back face — which made a card silently 602px inside a 289px column.
- `aria-controls` pointed at an id generated by `useId` and **attached to nothing**. Worse than
  omitting it: a screen reader follows the reference and finds nothing. Playwright's strict mode
  surfaced it.

A JSX comment written with `//` also rendered as visible text on the page — `{/* */}` or nothing.

**Motion degrades on its own.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is enforced globally, so the flip collapses
to an instant swap; verified at `1e-05s`, still flipping. The faces build their own surface rather
than nesting `Card`, because `backdrop-blur` and `transform-style: preserve-3d` interact badly — and
`Card`'s dark surface has one. **This is the first 3D transform in the codebase.**

## Drag to assign

`src/components/admin/team-assign.tsx` — the first **cross-container** drag here. Every existing
`DndContext` sorts one vertical list with `restrictToVerticalAxis`/`restrictToParentElement`, both of
which actively prevent moving an item between containers.

Two failures worth recording:

- **Pool cards were outside any `SortableContext`.** They rendered perfectly and simply could not be
  picked up, which looks identical to a broken drop target. Each container needs its own.
- **`closestCorners` never resolved across two columns.** The overlay followed the cursor while the
  target never lit up. `pointerWithin` asks the simpler and correct question — what is under the
  pointer — with `rectIntersection` as the fallback for the gap between two cards.

Also required: a `DragOverlay` (without it the card vanishes crossing the gap), `MeasuringStrategy.Always`
(zones change height as cards move), and `setActivatorNodeRef` so only the grip starts a drag.

**Drag is not the only way in.** Every card also has a plain button, because `block-list.tsx`
established that a drag interface here keeps a non-drag fallback.

## Taxonomy: merged, and sectors made real

`sectors` was **write-only** — 103 curated rows read by nothing outside its own admin screen, because
no persona had ever pointed at one. `resolve-layout.ts` documents an attempt to use the data anyway,
by flattening sector interaction modes up to the category; it was reverted because a five-sector
category nearly always contains one NARRATOR and one FAQ sector, so the rules fired for 20 of 20
categories. **The missing piece was the column.**

`personas.sector_id` (nullable, `SET NULL`) makes the card's third axis real. `/admin/categories` and
`/admin/sectors` now redirect to **`/admin/taxonomy`**, one screen showing categories with their
sectors nested and the suitability scores displayed where they are decided.

### A latent bug fixed on the way

`saveCategoryAction` regenerated the slug from the name on every save — and `CATEGORY_LAYOUT` and
`suggestedToolsFor` both key off that slug. **Renaming a category silently changed the chat layout
and suggested toolset for every persona in it.** The slug is now set once, on creation: it is a
behaviour contract, not a label.

## Not done

The **marketplace** still shows no persona visual at all — it was the one surface in the plan that
renders a plain card with no accent, initials or mark. It needs the card's data threaded through a
different query shape than the catalogue's, and belongs with work on the marketplace rather than
bolted on here.
